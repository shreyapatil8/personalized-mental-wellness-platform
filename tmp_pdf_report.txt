Abstract
College students contend with academic pressures, career and financial concerns, and feelings
of loneliness and emotional distress - but they also struggle to find timely support due to a
shortage of counsellors, affordability, stigma and reluctance to talk openly. AI-powered virtual
assistants can provide round-the-clock help but lack specificity of guidance and transitions to a
live counsellor as students get older.
We are solving these problems by building the Well Space: Personalised mental wellness
platform for college students that combines wellness profiling, ML-assisted counselling and AI
support for counsellors in one single platform for students, counsellors and administrators.
Students take a WHO-5 style wellbeing assessment, conduct daily mood check ins and write
journal entries to form a longitudinal wellness history chart.
In the server, the selected fresh consented records and fresh ML generation of the wellness-
risk category in Python scikit-learn, XGBoost and the newly generated LLM based content-
aware responses served through the Ollama API are streamed. An in-built counselling module
allows students to make counselling requests, optionally share their wellness summaries with
their consent and use the counsellor chat once approved. An administrator module allow
counsellor approval, user management, usage reports and user satisfaction tracking..
The system is developed using React.js (Vite), Tailwind CSS, Node.js, Express.js and MongoDB
Atlas and secures data by bcrypt password hashing, token authentication, role access, data
minimsation and voluntary action. This document describes the problem background, literature
review, objectives, scope, feasibility, methodology with UML diagrams, facilities needed,
project schedule, outputs achieved, test cases, conclusion, and future scope. The platform is a
wellness support platform and does not substitute for professional mental health care, diagnosis,
and emergency services.
Keywords: Mental Wellness, Artificial Intelligence, AI Wellness Assistant, Wellness
Intelligence Engine, Machine Learning, Natural Language Processing, Mood Tracking, Human
Counselling, Role-Based Access Control

Introduction
1.1 Introduction
The rapid proliferation of digital technology has presented a new channel for access to mental
wellness support. College students often experience academic pressures, career concerns, social
isolation, relationship challenges and general daily demands. Unfortunately, limited access to
professional counsellors, cost, societal stigma and discomfort to share their concerns often
prevents a young adult from getting proper and timely wellness support.
Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) allow us to create a system
that engages with students over a text conversation and can advise students on their overall
wellbeing. Responses can be generated instantly by AI assistants, but responses that don't take
account of a student's prior experiences might come across as inauthentic. Human counselling
allows for a more personal and professional response, but space, time and finances may make
this difficult to access. Mood tracking and journaling enable students to reflect, but the
information does not 'follow' the student if they move away from the assistant or counsellor.
The Well Space: Personalised mental wellness platform for college students combines wellness
profiling, ML-enabled wellness prediction and AI-supported counselling platform together in a
unified web-based platform. Some of the platform's key components are:
1. Secure User and Profile Management:
The platform helps the students, counsellors, and administrators in registration, login, roles
selection, and storing each student's wellness-related data.
2. Tracking Your Wellbeing:
Students also fill out a standard WHO-5 style wellbeing measure, check in their moods and
journal. All of this contributes to a longitudinal wellness history.
3. Context-Aware AI Wellness Assistant:
A locally hosted LLM served through the Ollama API, provides a warm, personalised reply that
brings together the most relevant and up-to-date records, the latest ML-based prediction of
wellness, and a Wellness Intelligence Engine (WIE).
4. Human Counselling Assistance:
Students can then set up appointments with counsellors, determine whether or not a wellness
summary can be shared with the counsellor, and chat with the counsellor once the request has
been made.
5. Dashboards & Admin:
A student dashboard displays wellness scores and history, while an administrator dashboard
supports counsellor approval, user management, usage reporting and user satisfaction.

1.4 Scope of Project
The scope of work includes the design and development of a web-based application that offers
personalised digital wellness support and access to human counselling for college students. The
platform is catered to three user roles: students, counsellors and administrators.
Key Modules and Functional Scope
 User Management and Wellness Profile: sign in, create an account, verify authenticity,
permission permissions and profile information.
 Wellness Tracking: recorded wellbeing evaluations, regular mood reviews and journal
entries as a history of health.
 Text-Based analysis: where used, NLP methods detect potential mood themes within text
submitted by students to help deliver tailored general wellbeing support. This is not a clinical
diagnosis.
 Wellness Prediction using ML: a Python based model generates an wellness-risk category
(Low, Medium or High) which is entered into the prediction history.
 AI Wellness Assistant: supportive text conversations produced by a LLM hosted locally on
Ollama using context chosen by the Wellness Intelligence Engine.
 Counselling Module: appointment requests, counsellor approval, sharing wellness summary
based on student consent, and student to counsellor chat.
 Wellness Dashboard: wellness scores and history of assessment results and wellness
summary of the student.
 Admin Module: counsellor request approval, user management, usage & reports, and user
satisfaction monitoring.
 Security and Privacy: hashed passwords, token-based sessions, access controls, role-based
permissions, minimisation of data, consent to share personal records.

The project focuses on accessible and personalised wellness support for students through AI

does not replace professional mental healthcare, clinical diagnosis, treatment or emergency
services. The features of the final system depend on the implementation completed during the

1.5 Problem Statement
Ideal Scenario
The ideal support system for college students' mental wellness offers personalized, real-time and
24/7 support throughout the wellness journey. It must take into account the wellness assessments,
mood logs, journal entries and previous conversations of the student to provide relevant
suggestions and help the student have seamless interaction with professional counsellors. It
should be able to keep track of the student's wellness context across times and allow counsellors
an efficient overview of students.
Most existing platforms offer online counselling and AI chatbot as independent features. An AI
chatbot might offer responses that are cookie cutter in nature and may not fully understand the
long-term context of the student, whereas a counsellor might require some time to understand
the student's context before providing relevant assistance. Current systems lack seamless
integration of wellness checks, AI support and counsellor meetings.
Consequences
The repetitive or less individualized assistance from apps can contribute to lower levels of
motivation and trust towards digital platforms for mental wellness. Counsellors may need to
allocate further time to go through a student's wellness progress, which leads to less effective use
of the counselling time and effort. The non-availability of wellness tracking makes it more
challenging for students and counsellors to follow individual progress and initiate relevant
actions.
Proposed Solution
To address these gaps, this project introduces a Personalized Mental Wellness Platform for
College Students – an integrated application that encompasses wellness assessment, AI-enabled
mental health support and engagement with a professional counsellor. The platform maintains a
comprehensive wellness profile of the user using wellness assessment, mood, journal entries, and

2.2 Research Gaps
From the methodologies mentioned above the following research gaps could be addressed:
1. Fragmented support options: AI help and human counselling typically exist within two
different flows rather than within one unified platform.
2. Need for customised interactions: generic responses by AI might not resemble the anxieties,
needs and wellness goals the user expresses.
3. Lack of continuity of support: users may need to repeat information when transitioning from
an AI assistant to a human counsellor.
4. Minimal linking of wellness tracking: mood logs and journal entries frequently do not link to
personalised feedback and summary of progress.
5. Emotion analysis issues: Text-based emotion analysis cannot always include context and
understanding, therefore responsible use and transparent limitations are important.
6. Requireevaluation: we cannot take for granted usability, relevance and usability of an
integrated AI and counselling platform.
2.3 Literature Review Analysis
Existing solutions to student wellness have some aspects of the problem, but they are not used
together. For example, a chatbot can give you on-demand answers that have not been informed by
prior interactions; a mood tracking tool can tell you how you're feeling without advising you; and
an online counselling service can begin a session without considering all previous digital
conversations.
In order to address these challenges the platform designs 1. Structured wellbeing assessment 2.
Mood and journal logging 3. ML based wellbeing prediction 4. Wellness Intelligence Engine for
context selection for the Ollama based assistant 5. Consent based counselling workflow with
admin dashboard. The responsible AI framework provided by the WHO and NIST has been
leveraged to define the privacy, consent and safety features. The gaps are minimized as all
techniques are supported by literature.

3.1 Project Feasibility
A feasibility study assesses whether the system is feasible (possible to develop) given the
technology, resources, schedule and budget. The overall feasibility of Mental Wellness Platform
is conducted from technical, operational and scheduling viewpoints.
1. Technical Feasibility :
Feasibility of the platform is not an issue, since the tools that would be used are the most
prominent web technologies, ML libraries, AI services and cloud platforms. Frontend:
React.js(Vite), Tailwind CSS, Node.js, Express.js (API), can be used for API Testing, real-time

wellness prediction model: scikit-learn, XGBoost, Python; ai conversation: ollama - local open-
source large language model available in local environment, openapi-driven; Development:
Postman, Git, GitHub, Vercel, Render, or Railway, Visual Studio Code.

The web application provides an easy, responsive frontend where students can register, take tests,
log moods, journals, communicate with the assistant and make requests for counselling.
Counselling requests are handled through the counsellors' dashboard and registrations and usage
are viewed from the admin panel. User authentication and role-specific access control provide
protection. As mental health is a sensitive topic, the application explains its limitations and does
not display model results or AI responses as diagnoses, students are advised to seek professional
counselling.

Cost is minimised since React.js, Node.js, Express.js, Python, scikit-learn and XGBoost are open
source with all development tools (Visual Studio Code, Git, GitHub, Postman) being free. Since
Ollama and the open source language models it runs are free there is no charge for API requests,
though costs for a computer with enough RAM (and preferably a GPU) to run the model, storage
on MongoDB Atlas, media storage, hosting and purchasing a domain name will have to be paid
for. Development can take place locally, using free tier cloud providers, before deploying using
other free tier cloud offerings. Costs should be checked against the current prices of each
provider. The project remains cost effective enough to work as a student level prototype.

Methodology
4.1 Proposed Methodology
Figure 5.1: Architecture of the Mental Wellness Platform
The proposed platform follows an incremental development approach in which the application is
divided into modules that are developed and tested separately before integration. The architecture
is modular: a React.js frontend communicates with a Node.js and Express.js backend; the
backend stores data in MongoDB Atlas, requests responses from the Ollama API and uses a
Python machine learning predictor (scikit-learn, XGBoost); Cloudinary provides media storage
where required; and the application is deployed using Vercel with Render or Railway. The
methodology consists of the following stages.
1. User Registration, Authentication and Profile Management :
The platform provides registration and login for students, counsellors and administrators.
– Passwords are hashed with bcrypt and never stored as plain text
– Session access uses token-based (JWT) authentication
– Role-based access control restricts students, counsellors and administrators to their own features
– Counsellor registrations are reviewed and approved by an administrator before support is enabled
2. Wellness Data Collection :
Students provide wellness information through three channels.
– A WHO-5 style wellbeing assessment of structured questions that produces a score and keeps an
assessment history
– Mood check-ins that record the student’s mood on a given day
– Journal entries in which students write personal reflections
– Inputs are validated before being sent to the backend and only necessary information is collected
3. ML-Based Wellness Prediction :
A Python component using scikit-learn and XGBoost classifies wellness-risk into Low, Medium
or High from assessment-derived features. Each prediction is stored with its timestamp in the
student’s prediction history. Predictions are decision-support information for personalisation and
Annasaheb Dange College of Engineering and Technology,Ashta 28 | P a g e

--- PAGE 31 ---

are never presented as clinical diagnoses.
4. Text Processing and Theme Identification :
Where implemented, text from journals and messages is cleaned and analysed to identify possible
emotional themes. The themes are used only to select and organise context for supportive
responses and for counsellor summaries, and are not clinical assessments.
5. Wellness Intelligence Engine (Context Selection) :
Before the AI assistant replies, the Wellness Intelligence Engine selects the information that is
relevant to the current message. It considers assessments, mood records, journal themes, the latest
ML prediction and earlier nversations, ranks them by relevance and recency, and applies a token
budget so that only the minimum necessary context is prepared. Only records for which the
student has given consent are used.
6.AI-Assisted Wellness Support :
The backend validates the student’s message, attaches the selected context and sends the prompt
to the Ollama API. The generated response is returned to the interface together with a reminder
that it is supportive guidance and not a diagnosis or a replacement for professional counselling.
Each exchange is stored in the student’s chat history, and temporary failures are retried a limited
number of times before a friendly message is shown.
7.Human Counselling Support :
Students request an appointment by choosing a counsellor, a future date and time, a topic and an
optional note. They may allow a wellness summary to be prepared for the counsellor; this
requires consent in the student’s profile. The counsellor approves or declines the request from
the counsellor dashboard. After approval, the student and counsellor can use the counselling chat
and shared documents. Appointment status is tracked as pending, approved, completed, cancelled
or rescheduled.
8.Dashboards, Administration and Evaluation :
Students view their score, assessment history and wellness overview. Administrators view total
students, active counsellors, pending counsellor requests, total users and feedback, manage user
accounts, and review usage reports and user satisfaction. The system is evaluated through
functional, integration and security testing, and usability assessment.

1.3 Conclusion and Future Scope
1.3.1 Conclusion
The Personalized Mental Wellness Platform for College Students integrates wellness profiling,
ML-based prediction and AI-assisted counselling support in a single web application. Students
can complete a WHO-5 style assessment, record moods and journals, hold context-aware
conversations with an AI wellness assistant, request counselling with consent-based sharing of a
wellness summary, and review their progress. Counsellors and administrators are supported
through approval workflows, user management, usage reports and satisfaction monitoring.
The use of a Wellness Intelligence Engine to select relevant and recent context, and of a locally
hosted LLM through the Ollama API to generate responses, aims to make support more
personalised than a generic chatbot, while consent, data minimisation, hashed passwords and
role-based access protect sensitive information. The platform is positioned as a complement to
professional mental health care and not as a diagnostic tool.
The report establishes the requirements, architecture, methodology, schedule and test plan, and
presents the implemented interfaces. The actual effectiveness and reliability of the platform
depend on the quality of AI responses, the accuracy of predictions and the outcome of testing
and user feedback; final claims about performance should be supported by measured results.
1.3.2 Future Scope
1. Advanced AI Conversations: more capable language models can give more natural,
context-aware conversations within appropriate safety boundaries.
2. Enhanced Personalisation: the recommendation logic can be enhanced by voting
preferences, assessment responses and feedback.
3. Supporting different languages: add multiple languages like Marathi, Hindi and local
languages to be able to reach more students.
4. Expanded Wellness Resources: guided breathing exercises, meditation resources,
relaxation techniques and educational material can be included.
5. Enhanced Human Counselling: counsellor availability calendars, appointment
reminders, session rescheduling and secure online counselling sessions can be introduced.
6. Subscription and Payment Support: if paid counselling services are required, subscription
plans and secure payment processing can be added.
7. Mobile Application: a dedicated mobile app can make the services more convenient to access.

1.5 Appendix
Appendix A: Responsible Use and Limitations
The Wellspace platform is a wellness-support tool. It does not diagnose mental health conditions,
does not provide treatment, and is not an emergency service. AI-generated responses and ML-
based wellness categories are supportive information only and may be incomplete or inaccurate.
Students who are feeling overwhelmed should be referred to health professionals or emergency
services. Wellness data is sensitive: it is gathered in small quantities, accessed via role based
authentication, and viewable by counsellors only with the student's permission.
